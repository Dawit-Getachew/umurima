Girinka, one cow per poor family, gives a dairy cow to a vulnerable household. The first female calf is passed on to another family in the community. Families are selected at village level, so ask your village leader, your Farmer Promoter or the sector veterinary officer how the selection works.

Zero grazing is the standard system in Rwanda. Cattle, inka, are kept in a shed and feed is carried to them, which protects hillsides from erosion and keeps manure where it can be collected. Build the shed with a dry floor, a roof, and a slope so urine and manure drain into a collection pit.

Feed dairy cattle napier grass as the base, plus legume fodder such as calliandra, leucaena or desmodium for protein, and always provide clean water. A dairy cow drinks a large amount of water every day and milk production drops sharply when water is short. Cut napier when it is about one metre high, before it becomes woody.

Ticks and tick borne diseases such as East Coast fever are a major cause of cattle death. Spray or dip animals on the schedule advised by the sector veterinary officer, keep the shed clean, and call the vet immediately if an animal has high fever, swollen glands or stops eating.

Goats, ihene, are cheap to keep, breed fast and suit small households. They need a raised slatted shed floor to stay dry, browse and cut fodder, salt, and regular deworming. Keep new animals separate for a few weeks before mixing them with the herd.

Poultry, inkoko, need a dry predator proof house, clean water at all times, and balanced feed. Newcastle disease can kill an entire flock within days, so vaccinate on the schedule your veterinary officer gives and repeat it as advised. Sick birds should be separated from the flock immediately.

Manure is a major benefit of keeping livestock. Compost manure with crop residues and kitchen waste before applying it to the field, because fresh manure can burn crops and carries weed seeds. Well composted manure improves soil structure and reduces the amount of mineral fertilizer needed.

Animal health questions such as illness, injury, difficult birth, abortion, or a sudden death need a veterinary officer, not an SMS. Contact your sector veterinary officer or RAB straight away, and isolate the sick animal from the rest of the herd until they arrive.
