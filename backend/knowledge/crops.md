Maize, ibigori in Kinyarwanda, is planted at the start of Season A and Season B. Common spacing is 75 cm between rows and 25 to 30 cm between plants, one or two seeds per hole. Use certified seed of a variety recommended for your altitude. Weed early, because weeds in the first six weeks cause the largest yield loss.

Beans, ibishyimbo, come in bush and climbing types. Climbing beans give much higher yields but need stakes and are best in the Northern and Western highlands. Bush beans mature faster and suit warmer, drier areas. Beans fix nitrogen, so they are a good rotation crop before or after maize.

Cassava, imyumbati, is planted from healthy stem cuttings about 25 cm long, pushed into ridges or mounds at an angle. Use only clean planting material from a certified source to avoid cassava mosaic and brown streak disease. Cassava tolerates poor soils and dry spells and can be harvested from about 9 to 12 months.

Irish potato, ibirayi, grows best in the cool highlands of Musanze, Nyabihu, Burera and Rubavu. Use certified seed potato, plant in ridges, and hill up the soil as the plants grow so tubers are not exposed and do not turn green. Late blight is the main disease in wet weather.

Rice, umuceri, is grown in marshland schemes under irrigation, usually through a cooperative. Transplant young seedlings in rows, keep water levels as the scheme manages them, and follow the cooperative planting calendar so water use is shared fairly.

Banana, urutoke or igitoke, is a perennial crop for food, beer and cash. Plant clean suckers or tissue culture plants, keep three plants per mat by removing extra suckers, and mulch heavily. Remove and destroy any plant showing wilt symptoms.

Coffee, ikawa, and tea, icyayi, are the main export crops. Coffee needs regular pruning, mulching and timely harvesting of only red ripe cherries, which is what earns the higher price. Tea is grown mainly in cooperatives near factories in the high rainfall zones. For both crops follow the guidance of NAEB and your cooperative.

Rotate crops rather than growing maize on the same plot every season. A common rotation is maize, then beans or soybean, then a root crop. Rotation breaks pest and disease cycles and legumes leave nitrogen in the soil for the next crop.

Intercropping maize with climbing beans or planting beans between banana mats is common and works when spacing is respected. Do not crowd plants, because too many plants on a small plot compete for water and nutrients and lower total yield.
