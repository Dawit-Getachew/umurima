NPK fertilizer contains nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium. The blend NPK 17-17-17 is widely used in Rwanda as a planting fertilizer for maize, beans and vegetables. Ifumbire is the Kinyarwanda word for fertilizer.

DAP is a phosphorus rich planting fertilizer. It is applied in the planting hole or furrow at sowing, mixed lightly with soil so the seed does not sit directly on the granules. Phosphorus helps root development early in the season.

Urea is a nitrogen fertilizer used for top dressing, not for planting. On maize it is usually applied when the crop is about knee high and again around tasselling, placed in a band beside the row and covered with soil. Apply on moist soil, because urea applied on dry soil is largely lost.

Yellow maize leaves: when the lower, older leaves of maize turn yellow from the tip along the middle vein, the crop is usually short of nitrogen. Top dress with urea on moist soil when the maize is knee high, and add compost or manure. If whole plants turn yellow after heavy rain, waterlogging may be the cause, so open drainage channels.

Do not apply urea to beans and other legumes. Legumes fix their own nitrogen through root nodules, so nitrogen fertilizer wastes money and can produce leafy plants with few pods.

Application rates depend on your soil, your crop and your plot size. RAB and the sector agronomist give recommended rates per hectare for each crop and zone. Ask your Farmer Promoter or agro dealer for the recommended rate for your crop and plot before buying, and follow the rate on the package.

Organic manure improves soil far more than fertilizer alone. Compost, farmyard manure from cattle or goats, and mulch add organic matter, hold water and feed soil life. The best results come from combining manure with a smaller amount of mineral fertilizer.

Many Rwandan soils are acidic, especially on worn hillsides. Acid soil locks up phosphorus so fertilizer gives a poor response. Agricultural lime, travertine, is used to correct acidity, and the amount needed depends on a soil test. Ask RAB or your sector agronomist about soil testing and lime for your plot.

Fertilizer and improved seed are distributed through registered agro dealers under the government subsidy program, ordered through the Smart Nkunganire System. Farmers register with their Farmer Promoter or cooperative before the season starts. Order early, because supply at the agro dealer is limited once planting begins.

Store fertilizer in a dry place off the ground and away from food, seed and animal feed. Keep bags closed, because urea and NPK absorb moisture from the air, harden into lumps and become difficult to spread evenly.
