Twigire Muhinzi is Rwanda's farmer to farmer extension system. It works through Farmer Promoters in every village and Farmer Field Schools where farmers learn by doing on a demonstration plot across a whole season. Extension advice is free.

A Farmer Promoter, umuhinzi mwitozwa, is a volunteer lead farmer chosen by the community and trained by RAB. The Farmer Promoter is the first person to ask about seed, fertilizer, planting advice, pests, and registering for the input subsidy. Ask at your village office or your cooperative for the name of your Farmer Promoter.

RAB, the Rwanda Agriculture and Animal Resources Development Board, is the national agency for agricultural research and extension. RAB develops and releases improved varieties, runs soil testing and plant clinics, and supports the veterinary services. Serious or spreading crop and animal problems should reach RAB through your sector agronomist.

MINAGRI, the Ministry of Agriculture and Animal Resources, sets national agricultural policy and runs the input subsidy program. NAEB, the National Agricultural Export Development Board, handles export crops such as coffee, tea and horticulture.

At sector level there is a sector agronomist and a sector veterinary officer working in the sector office. Go to the agronomist for crop, soil, seed and land use questions, and to the veterinary officer for animal disease, vaccination and breeding questions.

For seed, fertilizer and pesticide, buy from a registered agro dealer. Registered dealers sell approved products, can show you the label and expiry date, and are the point of sale for subsidised inputs ordered through the Smart Nkunganire System. Avoid unlabelled products sold in the market.

Cooperatives matter for marketing, storage and irrigation schemes. Selling through a cooperative usually gives a better price than selling individually at the farm gate, and it gives access to shared drying grounds, stores and equipment.

Ask for help early rather than after a crop is lost. A pest seen in the first week, a soil problem noticed before planting, or a sick animal reported on the first day can usually be handled. The same problem reported a month later often cannot.
