Fall armyworm attacks maize. The signs are ragged windows chewed in young leaves, wet sawdust like frass in the funnel of the plant, and later damage to the cob. Scout your field twice a week from emergence, because the pest is easiest to control when the larvae are small.

For fall armyworm, handpicking and crushing larvae on a small plot works well and costs nothing. Keep the field weed free, plant at the same time as your neighbours so the pest is not concentrated on late fields, and use push pull with desmodium and napier grass where it is available. If you need a pesticide, ask your Farmer Promoter or agro dealer which product is approved and use exactly the rate written on the label.

Banana bacterial wilt, BXW, causes yellowing and wilting leaves, premature and uneven ripening of fruit, and yellow bacterial ooze when the stem is cut. Remove the male bud with a forked stick as soon as the last hand has formed, cut out and bury infected plants, and disinfect every tool with fire or bleach between plants. The disease spreads on dirty tools and insects moving between mats.

Cassava mosaic disease shows as yellow and green mottled leaves that are twisted and reduced in size, and it strongly reduces root yield. It spreads through infected cuttings and by whiteflies. Plant only certified clean cuttings and tolerant varieties, uproot and destroy badly affected plants, and never take cuttings from a diseased field.

Striga, a purple flowering witchweed, is a parasitic weed of maize and sorghum that attaches to the roots and starves the crop. It is worst on poor, exhausted soils. Pull and destroy the weed before it flowers and sets seed, add manure and compost, and rotate with legumes such as beans, soybean or desmodium which reduce the striga seed bank.

Storage pests such as the maize weevil and the larger grain borer destroy grain after harvest. Dry the grain properly until it is hard, clean and sort it, and store it in sealed hermetic bags or a clean airtight container. Clean the store before harvest and do not put new grain on top of old infested grain.

Late blight in Irish potato appears as dark water soaked patches on leaves that spread quickly in cool wet weather and rot the tubers. Use certified seed potato, plant with proper spacing so air moves through the crop, remove infected plants and do not leave cull piles near the field.

Never invent a pesticide dose and never mix products at a stronger rate than the label says. Wrong doses harm you, the crop, the soil and bees, and they make pests resistant. Read the label, wear protection, respect the waiting period before harvest, and ask your Farmer Promoter, agro dealer or RAB when you are not sure.

If a problem is spreading fast, is new to your area, or affects many farms at once, report it to your Farmer Promoter or sector agronomist so RAB can respond. Early reporting protects the whole community.
